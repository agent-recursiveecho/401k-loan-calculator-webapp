Model assumptions and limitations

The 26-paycheck projection is not necessarily the same as a 26-payment loan period. The calculator treats a financed fee as part of amortized principal, uses a nominal annual rate divided by 26, rounds loan payments to cents, and adjusts the final installment. The employer may calculate loan interest daily or differently. Loan proceeds are counted once as cash inflow and a loan-funded rent payment is still a cash expense; there is no duplicate “freed cash” added to income. This model does not include investment performance, future pay changes, benefit changes, other taxes, early loan repayment, true-up rules, annual IRS contribution limits, or an assumption that the employer matches above the stated cap. The net paycheck estimate assumes additional pre-tax contributions reduce income tax withholding at the user-entered marginal rate; Roth contributions do not. Verify all loan terms, payroll cutoffs, eligibility, and applicable tax rules with the plan administrator. This is an educational planning tool, not financial or tax advice.
